## 1. Overview

The implementation in PR #2 conforms closely to the technical spec. The review found two defects in the spec itself
(FX-01, FX-02), one gap in config fail-safe behaviour (FX-03), and a set of verification checks that are weaker than
the behaviour they gate (FX-04–FX-09). FX-10 records implementation choices the PR made that the spec did not settle.
§3 lists code-only fixes that need no spec change.

**Product impact.** None. No fix changes approved player-visible behaviour, scope, or an explicitly fixed product
value. FX-01 restores the approved behaviour (CE-2, §3.4.7 "dragging is exactly 1-to-1") in a case the spec text
broke.

**Compatibility.** Seeded generation is unchanged: `ValleyGenerator`, `world.json`, and the golden hashes
(`Seed1 = 0x9498738BB747720D`, `Seed2 = 0xF28BBFBF082B3E56`, `Seed20261127 = 0x6ABC5BEA7DE2822D`) stay as they are.
`camera.json` and `world.json` formats are unchanged. FX-04 may reject delivered art that passed before (see FX-04).

**Dev controls.** Still none. FX-05 narrows the test-only stub mode of `tools/verify-all.sh`; it is not a game
control.

## 2. Spec fixes

### FX-01 · Zoom anchor must not survive a drag (§3.4.4)

**Problem.** Step 4 captures a zoom anchor on every scroll with the pointer in the window, even while a drag is
active. Step 7 applies the drag constraint while dragging and clears the anchor only in its non-drag branch. An
anchor captured mid-drag therefore survives the drag. On the release tick, step 5 ends the drag and step 7 then takes
the anchor branch: `C = A − (sx·W/2, sy·H/2)` with the pointer position from the scroll. The view jumps back by
`(s_release − s_scroll)·W/2`, undoing the drag made after the scroll, and the release glide velocity is overwritten.
This violates CE-2 and §3.4.7. `CameraRig.cs:245-258` and `:388-397` implement the spec text exactly.

**Fix.** While a drag is active, a scroll changes only `Ht`. The drag constraint already keeps the grabbed point `G`
under the pointer while `H` eases to `Ht`, so the zoom is still about the cursor, with no anchor needed.

Replace steps 4 and 5 of §3.4.4 with:

> 4. **Scroll** (`n = clamp(ScrollNotches, -4, 4)`, `n ≠ 0`): `Ht = clamp(Ht × f, hMinEff, hMaxEff)` with
>    `f = 1 / (1 + step·n)` for `n > 0` (zoom in) and `f = 1 + step·(−n)` for `n < 0`, `step = zoomStepPct / 100`.
>    The anchor is resolved in step 5a.
> 5. **Drag start** (new drag, pointer in window): drag becomes active, velocity `= 0`, anchor cleared; the grab point
>    is `G = P(sx, sy)`. **Drag end** (drag active, button no longer effectively held): velocity `=` drag velocity
>    limited to `2 × panSpeedPerViewHeight × H`; anchor cleared.
>
>    5a. **Zoom anchor** (only on a tick with `n ≠ 0`): if a drag is active after step 5, the anchor is cleared (the
>    drag constraint keeps `G` under the pointer). Otherwise, if the pointer is in the window, the plane point
>    `A = P(sx, sy)` and `(sx, sy)` become the zoom anchor; otherwise the anchor is cleared (zoom about the screen
>    centre).

Add to step 7: *Invariant: the anchor is never set while a drag is active.*

**Edge cases.**

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Scroll on the tick a drag starts | Drag is active after step 5, so no anchor. `G` stays under the pointer |
| Scroll on the tick a drag ends | No drag after step 5, so the anchor is captured at the release pointer. The glide starts from the anchored pose. A key axis or a new drag clears the anchor as before |
| Scroll mid-drag, keep dragging, release | No anchor exists at release. The pose is continuous and the glide velocity is the limited drag velocity |
| Anchor set before the drag starts | Cleared at drag start (unchanged) |

**Verification.** Extend **T-CORE-22**: press drag at NDC `(0.2, -0.3)` with `H = 44`; scroll `+1` at `(0.2, -0.3)`;
move the pointer to `(-0.5, 0.4)` over 30 ticks; release.
- At the end of every tick while dragging (unclamped), the grabbed point stays under the pointer within `1e-3` m.
- On the release tick, `C` changes by at most `|V_release|·dt + 1e-3` m.
- After the release tick the rig has no zoom anchor.
- Repeat with the scroll on the drag-start tick and on the release tick (expected results per the table above).

### FX-02 · Plunge pool inside the T-CORE-08 bank band (§3.1.5, §6.2)

**Problem.** The last clause of T-CORE-08 requires every vertex between `HalfWidth + 300` and `HalfWidth + 600` cm
from the stream centreline to have `height ≥ level + 15`. Near the plunge pool, the stream-pass carving correctly
picks the pool as the nearest candidate. It carves the pool bed to `plungeLevel − 70` within 2 m of the pool edge and
shapes the shore to `plungeLevel + 50` within 6 m of it. The pool's own bed and shore overlap the stream's bank band
there, so the clause cannot hold for every seed. Without an exemption, 3,366 vertices fail across the 66-seed sweep,
all within 3 m of the plunge-pool edge.

The implementation narrowed the test (`tests/Core/World/WorldWaterTests.cs:109-110`, skip when `poolE < 300`) instead
of reporting the conflict, as ARCH-002 requires. The generator is correct: the pool is meant to be a basin.

**Fix.** Keep the generator and golden hashes unchanged and make the check say what the design intends. In §6.2,
replace the last clause of T-CORE-08 with:

> every vertex between `HalfWidth + 300` and `HalfWidth + 600` cm from the stream centreline, and at least `300` cm
> outside the `PlungePool` edge (`Isqrt(|p − centre|²) − 800 ≥ 300`), has height `≥ level + 15`; every vertex
> between `300` and `600` cm outside the `PlungePool` edge has height `≥ plungeLevel + 15`

Add one sentence to §3.1.5 after the carving block:

> Within 3 m of the `PlungePool` edge the pool's bed and shore take precedence over the stream banks. The pool is a
> basin, and its own shore rule (T-CORE-08) applies there.

**Verification.** T-CORE-08 with both clauses, over the existing sweep seeds and the range-corner configs. If the new
pool-shore clause fails for any seed, stop and report it to the architect with the seed and vertex. Do not adjust
values or narrow the check.

### FX-03 · Config read errors fall back instead of throwing (§2.5, §2.6)

**Problem.** §2.5 covers missing, oversized, malformed, schema, and range failures, but not a file that exists and
cannot be read (locked, access denied, I/O error). `JsonConfigLoader.cs:198` (`File.ReadAllBytes`) can throw
`IOException` or `UnauthorizedAccessException`. Nothing catches it, so `GameBootstrap.Awake` aborts and no world is
built. §2.5 also says a disallowed file name is "refused" but gives no reason code (the code logs
`<disallowed> … (invalid)`).

**Fix.** In §2.5, insert after rule 2 and amend rule 1:

> 1. `fileName` must be one of the two literal names above; otherwise the loader does no file access and logs
>    `[Murim] Config <disallowed> ignored (invalid); using defaults.` (the caller's string is never echoed). …
>
> 2a. Any `IOException`, `UnauthorizedAccessException`, or `System.Security.SecurityException` thrown while probing
>    or reading the file → reason `unreadable`. Only the exception's type name may be logged (§2.6), and it is not
>    required.

Add `unreadable` and `invalid` to the reason codes in the §2.6 `Config fallback` row.

**Verification.** Extend **T-ENG-01**: add file-access-seam cases where the probe throws `IOException`, and where
`ReadBytes` throws `IOException` and `UnauthorizedAccessException`. Each must return `false` and log exactly one
`[Murim] Config <file> ignored (unreadable); using defaults.` warning. No exception may escape, and the existing
no-path / no-user / no-machine-name assertion applies to these lines too.

### FX-04 · Art format checks and safe PNG parsing (§4, §6.4)

**Problem.** §4 requires "PNG, 8 bits per channel", but T-ART-01 and T-ART-02 do not list bit depth or colour type.
The implementation checks them only when it decodes pixels. RGB tiles without a bottom-row rule skip that path
(`ArtDeliveryTests.cs:39-40`), so a 16-bit or palette tile passes. `PngReader` also trusts the IHDR width and height:
`Width × bpp` and `Height × (stride + 1)` can overflow `int`, and zlib output is unbounded. Malformed input then throws
instead of being reported by ART ID and file name, as ARCH-004 requires.

**Fix.** In §4 *Common rules → Format*, append: *non-interlaced; colour type 2 (RGB) where RGB is specified and 6
(RGBA) where RGBA is specified; no palette or greyscale PNGs.*

Add an *Art validation input rules* paragraph to §4 (ARCH-004):

> The art suite treats committed PNGs as untrusted input. `PngReader` reads only the signature, chunk headers, and
> IHDR until the IHDR checks pass. Width and height must equal the contracted size from `ArtManifest` (and are
> therefore positive and ≤ 8192) before any IDAT data is decompressed. Size arithmetic uses `long`. Decompressed output
> is capped at exactly `height × (width × bpp + 1)` bytes: more data is an error. Every parse failure becomes an
> `ArtValidationIssue` (`ValidPng`) naming the ART ID and file name. No exception escapes a test.

Add to **T-ART-01** and **T-ART-02**: *IHDR bit depth `8`, interlace `0`, colour type per §4 Format, checked for every
file regardless of which pixel rules apply.* Add to **T-ART-05** (rule self-tests): a 16-bit RGB tile, a palette tile,
an interlaced file, an IHDR larger than its contract, and a truncated or oversized IDAT stream. Each yields one issue
and no exception.

**Owner note.** The tightened check runs against the 38 delivered PNGs. If any fails, that is an art redelivery
item for the owner, reported by ART ID. Implementers do not convert or substitute art.

### FX-05 · Verification tooling cannot fake a pass (§3.6.3, §6.5)

**Problem.**
- `MURIM_VERIFY_STUB_MODE=pass` left in a shell makes a plain `tools/verify-all.sh` exit `0` having run nothing. Any
  other value skips the missing-tool exit-`2` check.
- A Unity run that executes zero tests (for example an `--filter` that matches nothing) reports success.
- Stale `artifacts/` and `Builds/` contents can satisfy the T-BLD-02 file checks.
- The T-BLD-02 user/machine-name check is a case-insensitive substring match. A short user name such as `ab` matches
  the hex hash and fails the build spuriously.

**Fix.** Add to §3.6.3:

> - Stub mode is test-only. The script honours `MURIM_VERIFY_STUB_MODE` only when it is invoked by `--self-test` with
>   the internal argument `--self-test-child`. If the variable is set on any other invocation, the script prints
>   `MURIM_VERIFY_STUB_MODE is set outside --self-test` and exits `2`.
> - Each Unity `-runTests` invocation must produce its results XML, and that XML must report at least one test case;
>   otherwise exit `1`. Pass/fail still comes from Unity's exit code and the XML `result` attribute (`Passed`).
> - `--suite build` deletes `Builds/Windows/` before building. Each suite deletes its own results files under
>   `artifacts/` before running.

In §6.5 **T-BLD-02**: match user and machine names only as whole tokens, bounded by the start or end of the line or
any character outside `[A-Za-z0-9]`. Skip names shorter than 3 characters for the token match; the `/` and `\` checks
already cover paths.

Extend **T-TOOL-01**:
- `MURIM_VERIFY_STUB_MODE=pass tools/verify-all.sh --suite core` exits `2`.
- A stub Unity run that writes a results XML with zero test cases makes the script exit `1`.
- A stub run that writes no results XML makes the script exit `1`.

### FX-06 · T-ENG-12 tests the shipped overlay, not a copy (§6.3)

**Problem.** The engine suite runs PlayMode with `-nographics`, where the screen size cannot change. The
implementation runs the behaviour cycle once at the headless size and checks layout at the three listed sizes on a
second prefab instance switched to `ScreenSpaceCamera`. That is not the shipped Screen Space Overlay canvas, and
T-ENG-12 as written cannot be met headless.

**Fix.** Split T-ENG-12 into behaviour and layout, both on the instance `GameBootstrap` creates:

> **T-ENG-12** | 008 | PlayMode with `InputTestFixture`, on the overlay instance created by `GameBootstrap` at the
> runner's screen size: press `Esc`; hold a pan key, scroll, right-drag, press `Home`; click `Resume`; reopen and press
> `Esc`; reopen and click `Quit` with `QuitAction` replaced | overlay active with title text `Paused` and buttons
> `Resume`, `Quit`; camera pose unchanged while open; closes on `Resume` and on `Esc`, and camera input works on the
> next frame; `QuitAction` invoked exactly once; `Time.timeScale` stays 1. **Layout (same instance, no rendering):**
> canvas `ScreenSpaceOverlay`; `CanvasScaler` *Scale With Screen Size*, reference 1920 × 1080, *Shrink*; panel,
> title, divider, and button `RectTransform`s centre-anchored with the sizes and offsets of §3.5.3; for screen sizes
> 1920 × 1080, 960 × 540, and 800 × 1200, the scale factor `s = min(w/1920, h/1080)` keeps the panel
> `(520·s × 420·s)` fully inside the screen with its centre at the screen centre (computed, within 1 px)

The rendered look at real window sizes stays in PT-6 (resize with the overlay open) and PT-7 (960 × 540 and
maximised). No second prefab instance is created.

### FX-07 · Scroll delta behaviour is a project setting (§3.4.2, §6.3)

**Problem.** §3.4.2 relies on the project's Input System setting *Scroll Delta Behavior*, but the repository has no
Input System settings asset. `CameraRigDriver.cs:41-47` changes the global `InputSystem.settings` at runtime instead.
The related assertion (`CameraDriverTests.cs:52-54`) reads the fixture's fresh settings before the scene loads, so it
proves nothing.

**Fix.** In §3.6.1, add: *Input System settings asset `Assets/Settings/InputSystem.inputsettings.asset`, registered
as the project's Input System settings (`EditorBuildSettings` config object `com.unity.input.settings`), with Scroll
Delta Behavior = Uniform Across All Platforms.*

In §3.4.2, add: *`CameraRigDriver` never writes `InputSystem.settings`.* The `scroll.y / 120` fallback is removed,
because the pinned package has the setting.

Add to **T-ENG-02**: the registered Input System settings asset exists and its scroll delta behaviour is *Uniform
Across All Platforms*; no `.cs` file under `Assets/Scripts/` assigns `InputSystem.settings`. **T-ENG-08** keeps
"one detent = one 15 % step" and drops the pre-scene settings assertion.

### FX-08 · Flora fade material and batching claim (§3.3.4, §9)

**Problem.**
- §3.3.4 sets `_Fade` on `WorldFlora.mat`. In the Editor that dirties a committed asset on every zoom. The
  implementation (`PropView.cs:218-231`) drives one runtime copy, which is the better choice but is unrecorded.
- The budget paragraph says Unity "batches consecutive sprites". In fact flora (`WorldFlora`) and all other props
  (`WorldSprite`) interleave in one back-to-front order, so every material switch breaks a batch. At default zoom,
  with flora visible, that can be thousands of batch breaks.

**Fix.** In §3.3.4, replace "sets `_Fade` … on `WorldFlora.mat`" with:

> sets `_Fade` on one runtime copy of `WorldFlora.mat`, created by `PropView.Build` and shared by every flora
> renderer (the asset on disk keeps `_Fade = 1`)

Replace the batching sentence of the budget paragraph with:

> Sprites come from one atlas page. Consecutive renderers that share a material batch; flora and other props
> interleave in sort order, so batches break at each material switch while flora is visible (view height < 64 m). At
> overview zoom the flora root is inactive and the remaining props share `WorldSprite`.

Add a §9 risk row:

> | Flora/prop batch breaks | Accepted for `v0.1.0`; PT-2 and PT-3 judge smoothness at default zoom on the release build. If they show frame drops, the levers in order are `floraDensityPct` (`world.json`), then moving flora onto `WorldSprite` with the fade in vertex alpha (a later spec). |

**Verification.** Add to **T-ENG-06**: every flora renderer's `sharedMaterial` is the same instance, it is not the
`WorldFlora.mat` asset, and the asset's `_Fade` stays `1` after `ApplyViewHeight(57)`.
